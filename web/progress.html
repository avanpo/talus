How We Measure Cardiovascular Endurance & Fitness Progress

Traditional pace tracking is easily distorted by weather, elevation, and acute fatigue. Talus measures physiological progress using second-by-second telemetry, heart rate efficiency, and fatigue-normalized segment comparisons developed for endurance trail and road athletes.

Aerobic Efficiency Factor (EF)

Formula: Speed (m/min) ÷ Average HR (bpm)
EF reflects cardiovascular stroke volume — how much physical ground you cover per single heartbeat. As aerobic mitochondrial base builds, you cover more distance per beat. An upward trend over weeks confirms genuine aerobic adaptation.

Aerobic Decoupling (Pa:Hr)

Formula: (EF 1st Half − EF 2nd Half) ÷ EF 1st Half
Decoupling measures cardiac drift during steady-state runs. A drift under 5% confirms strong aerobic stamina with minimal fatigue. A drift > 5% indicates acute fatigue, dehydration, thermal strain, or exceeding your current aerobic threshold.

Grade-Adjusted Pace (GAP)

Model: Minetti et al. metabolic energy cost curves.
Trail elevation makes raw pace comparisons meaningless. GAP converts climbing energy (1m ascent ≈ 7.5m flat distance) into the equivalent flat-road speed, allowing accurate fitness comparisons regardless of vertical gain.

Fatigue & Load Normalization

Model: In-Run Position + Acute 7-Day Training Load.
Running a segment at km 15 of a long run during an 80km training week is vastly harder than on fresh legs. Talus normalizes segment scores based on prior 7-day cumulative load and pre-segment distance within the workout.
